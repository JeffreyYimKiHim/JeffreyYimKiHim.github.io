A thesis held in the head is a liquid instrument: it reprices continuously, its terms renegotiate themselves, and it never, ever marks to market. Memory edits the entry price.

Writing fixes the terms. A paragraph committed to a page is a contract with a past self — the version of me that had the idea gets frozen at the moment of maximum conviction, and the version of me reading it six months later gets to audit him.

This is why I treat writing as the cheapest backtest available. It costs an evening, it requires no data license, and it exposes the two failures that no quantitative test can catch: the thesis that was never falsifiable, and the reasoning that was never actually reasoning.

The second reason is stranger. Explaining an idea to an imagined reader — especially a skeptical one — recruits a different part of the brain than holding it. Ideas that feel load-bearing in the skull turn out to be decorative on paper. Better here than in the book.

So this site is not a publication. It's a forcing function with a URL.
