Jeffrey Yim

Multi-asset execution trader, working toward a PM seat. I write about AI-augmented investing — how machines can surface, structure, and stress-test ideas — and about the AI-to-human interface: how context gets presented so that decisions under limited information get better, not noisier.

Lately I've been contextualizing fixed income market microstructure with AI tooling, and sketching app blueprints for problems I keep running into outside markets.

Everything here is a working note, not investment advice. Views are my own.

Beliefs

  • Execution is information; most desks throw it away.
  • AI's edge is not prediction — it's compression of context.
  • Limited information is the default state; design for it.
  • A trade idea is only as good as its kill criteria.
  • Microstructure is macro, one order at a time.
  • The best interface makes the human smarter, not faster.
  • Post-mortems compound; P&L alone doesn't.
  • Conviction should be sized, not felt.
  • Liquidity is a promise that expires.
  • Writing is the cheapest backtest.
